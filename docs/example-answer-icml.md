## Picks, best first

The file has no session times, so these are ranked only. I can't group them by session or make a calendar file. Each reason is based on the first 700 characters of the abstract.

- **#1 [Provable Bounds for the Learnability of Sample-Compressible Families from Noisy Samples](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/62572)**: Arefe Boushehrian, Amir Najafi. It asks when a family of distributions on R^d can be learned at all, and with how many samples, when the samples are noisy (the noise appears in the title; the part I read covers sample complexity). This is the learning-theory side of your finite-sample bounds for recovering a latent density from noisy data.
- **#2 [Normalization-equivariant Diffusion Models: Learning Posterior Samplers From Noisy And Partial Measurements](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63341)**: Brett Levac, Jon Tamir, Marcelo Pereyra, Julián Tachella. It trains a generative prior from noisy and partial measurements only, without clean data, and uses it for posterior sampling. This is the imaging version of your plan: learn the latent distribution from noisy data, then use it as the prior for denoising.
- **#3 [Sharp Inequalities between Total Variation and Hellinger Distances for Gaussian Mixtures](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/62204)**: Joonhyuk Jung, Chao Gao. A Gaussian location mixture is exactly what you observe in Gaussian-noise deconvolution, with the mixing distribution as your latent law. Their sharp bounds between TV and Hellinger distance for such mixtures bear on how a good fit of your convolved model in one distance carries over to another.
- **#4 [Noise-Robust Density Estimation for Tabular Data Anomaly Detection](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/63974)**: Dazhi Fu, Zhao Zhang, Jicong Fan. It aims to estimate the density of the clean data with the noise's effect removed, a deconvolution goal, here for anomaly detection in tabular data. It could be an application or a baseline for your method.
- **#5 [Ambient Dataloops: Generative Models for Dataset Refinement](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/64608)**: Adrian Rodriguez-Munoz, William Daspit, Adam Klivans, Antonio Torralba, Constantinos Daskalakis, Giannis Daras. It treats samples as noisy at a set noise level and uses Ambient Diffusion to learn the underlying distribution. This is a deep-generative way to learn a distribution from corrupted samples, to set beside your matching of the noise-convolved model to the data.
- **#6 [Learning Normalized Energy Models for Linear Inverse Problems](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/64230)**: Nicolas M Zilberstein, Santiago Segarra, Eero Simoncelli, Florentin Guth. It learns an explicit, normalized prior density by training for denoising, then computes posterior densities across measurement conditions. This matches your use of the learned density as an empirical prior for posterior denoising.
- **#7 [Fast and Scalable Analytical Diffusion](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/64125)**: Xinyi Shang, Peng Sun, Jingyu Lin, Zhiqiang Shen. It writes the denoising score as an empirical-Bayes posterior mean with the training data as the prior. It is the same posterior-mean denoising as yours, except your prior is a learned density.
- **#8 [(De)-regularized Maximum Mean Discrepancy Gradient Flow](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/68702)**: Zonghao Chen, Aratrika Mustafi, Pierre Glaser, Anna Korba, Arthur Gretton, Bharath K. Sriperumbudur. It proposes a modified MMD whose gradient flow has a closed form using only samples and converges near-globally. That matters for how an MMD-type objective like your convMMD behaves when you optimize it.
- **#9 [Minimum Distance Summaries for Robust Neural Posterior Estimation](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/61901)**: Sherman Khoo, Dennis Prangle, Song Liu, Mark Beaumont. It applies minimum-distance summaries (its abstract matches the MMD terms) to likelihood-free, simulation-based inference to make it robust to misspecification. It draws on the same distance-matching tools as your likelihood-free convMMD estimator.
- **#10 [Transformers Learn the Optimal DDPM Denoiser for Multi-Token GMMs](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/66142)**: Hongkang Li, Hancheng Min, Rene Vidal. It analyses training a denoiser on Gaussian-mixture data and shows convergence to the optimal one. That is the denoising problem under your Gaussian-mixture sieve.
- **#11 [Mixtures Closest To A Given Measure: A Semidefinite Programming Approach](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/66790)**: Srećko Ðurašinović, Jean B Lasserre, Victor Magron. It picks the number of mixture components and their parameters to approximate a measure in W2 or TV distance. This is the approximation side of your Gaussian-mixture sieve.
- **#12 [Stationary MMD Points](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/62538)**: Zonghao Chen, Toni Karvonen, Heishiro Kanagawa, Francois-Xavier Briol, Chris J Oates. MMD objectives are non-convex, so it studies the stationary points that optimization actually reaches rather than global minimizers. That speaks to the gap between your theory for empirical sieve minimizers and what gradient training finds.
- **#13 [Flow Matching Calibration for Simulation-Based Inference under Model Misspecification](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/64710)**: Pierre-Louis Ruhlmann, Michael Arbel, Florence Forbes, Pedro Luiz Coelho Rodrigues. It corrects simulation-trained posteriors when the simulator, noise or prior model is wrong, using a small calibration set. This is relevant if the measurement-error model you assume in deconvolution is wrong.
- **#14 [One-shot Conditional Sampling: MMD meets Nearest Neighbors](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/66688)**: Anirban Chatterjee, Sayantan Choudhury, Rohan Hore. It trains a conditional generator with an MMD loss, including for approximate posterior sampling in simulation-based inference. It is a close cousin of your MMD-trained generator used for posterior denoising.
- **#15 [Diffusion Models Are Statistically Optimal for Learning Low-Dimensional Multi-Modal Distributions](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/64667)**: Jingda Wu, Changxiao Cai. It gives sample-complexity results for learning distributions with a deep generative model without strong smoothness assumptions. Its rates are a point of comparison for your L2 rates under Sobolev smoothness.
- **#16 [Kernel-based Maximum-of-difference Test for Two-sample Comparison](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/65602)**: Dan Pu, Tianyi Zhu, Yao Yan, Wei Lan. It shows that MMD can perform poorly against covariance and location shifts and proposes a maximum-of-difference alternative. Worth knowing for the discrepancy your convMMD loss is built on.
- **#17 [Are Two Datasets Close Enough With Statistical Significance? A Kernel Distributional Closeness Testing Approach](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/62769)**: Zhijian Zhou, Liuhua Peng, Xunye Tian, Mingming Gong, Feng Liu. It is an MMD-based test of whether two distributions are within ε of each other. You could use it to check whether your fitted noise-convolved model is close enough to the observed data.
- **#18 [Diffusion posterior sampling for simulation-based inference in tall data settings](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/68813)**: Julia Linhart, Gabriel Cardoso, Alexandre Gramfort, Sylvain Le Corff, Pedro Luiz Coelho Rodrigues. It does scientific inference with intractable likelihoods from many independent observations using score-based models. This is the scientific-inference setting that motivates your work.
- **#19 [Dimension-free convergence of diffusion models for approximate Gaussian mixtures](https://ICML.cc/virtual/2026/poster/65046)**: Gen Li, Changxiao Cai, Yuting Wei. It gives theory for denoising-based sampling when the data are close to a Gaussian mixture, the latent class your Gaussian-mixture sieve uses.
