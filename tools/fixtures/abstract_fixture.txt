We study how to learn a distribution when every observation is corrupted by noise of known form. Instead of fitting a parametric model, we estimate the latent distribution nonparametrically by matching the noisy data to samples from a generator with a kernel discrepancy, which avoids density estimation and works with small samples. We prove consistency of the estimator, show that the discrepancy can be optimised by stochastic gradients, and evaluate on synthetic benchmarks and a scientific imaging dataset, where it recovers sharper latent structure than deconvolution baselines.
